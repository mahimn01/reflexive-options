## Setup

For the closed-form log-normal-OI parameterisation
(\cref{sec:hopf-closed-form}), the Routh--Hurwitz polynomial $H(\kappa) = c_1 c_2 - c_0$
collapses to a quadratic in $\kappa$ with coefficients $(A_2, A_1, A_0)$ that
depend on the partials $(G_y, G_v)$ and the deterministic triangle
$(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \kappa_v)$:
\begin{equation*}
A_2 = G_y^2 (\alpha + \kappa_v), \quad
A_1 = G_v \beta\gamma - G_y(\alpha + \kappa_v)^2, \quad
A_0 = \alpha\kappa_v(\alpha + \kappa_v) - \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\gamma.
\end{equation*}
The discriminant $D := A_1^2 - 4 A_2 A_0$ partitions $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$-space.
Define the operational \emph{no-Hopf wedge}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}} \;:=\; \{(\sigma_q, \gamma) : H(\kappa) = 0 \text{ has no positive root in } \kappa \in [0,\infty)\},
\label{eq:no-hopf-wedge}
\end{equation}
generated by two sub-cases: (i) the strict §3.5 wedge $D < 0$, or
(ii) $D \geq 0$ with both real roots non-positive. At the canonical specification
$(\mu_q = \log 100,\, T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.25,\, \kappa_v = 2,\, \alpha = 0.05,\, \beta = 1,\, \theta_v = 0.04)$
the strict-$D<0$ case is empty on the §3.7 canonical scan window; the wedge is
populated only via (ii), driven by $G_y < 0$ at the ATM-anchored equilibrium and
the small-$\gamma$ baseline-stability constraint $H(0) = A_0 > 0
\Leftrightarrow \gamma < 2 \alpha\kappa_v(\alpha + \kappa_v)/\beta \approx 0.41$.

\Cref{thm:bt-empty} addressed \emph{one} of the four possible codim-1 mechanisms
inside $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$ — the saddle-node $c_0(\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{SN}}) = 0$ —
and reported $\kappa_{\mathrm{SN}}(\sigma_q, \gamma) < 0$ at every cell of the §3.7
scan window: the saddle-node curve is unphysical. The open question this section
closes is: \emph{given the BT-empty result and the no-Hopf wedge, is there ANY
codim-1 bifurcation accessible as $\kappa$ ramps up from $0$ inside
$\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$?}

## Why the §3.7 BT-empty result is not the full picture

The §3.7 closed form solves $c_0(\kappa) = 0$ for the saddle-node via
$\det J(\kappa) = 0$. Two further codim-1 routes to instability remain available:
(a) the Hopf itself $H(\kappa) = 0$ — which the wedge definition rules out by
construction; (b) a trace flip $c_2(\kappa) = -\kappa G_y + (\alpha + \kappa_v) = 0$,
crossing a real eigenvalue from the LHP to the RHP not through zero (the
$c_2 = 0$ route is a saddle-node-of-a-different-coordinate-chart whenever
$\partial_\kappa \det J \ne 0$ at the crossing). Inside $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$
the wedge precludes (a), but neither §3.5 nor §3.7 rules out (b) directly.

## Theorem 6

\begin{theorem}[No-Hopf-wedge taxonomy]
\label{thm:no-hopf-wedge}
Let $(\sigma_q, \gamma) \in \mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$ at the canonical
specification, and let the equilibrium partials $(G_y, G_v)$ be the
log-normal-OI values at $(a^\star, v^\star) = (\mu_q, \theta_v)$. Assume the
sufficient conditions
\begin{align*}
\text{(S1)} \quad & G_y \leq 0, \\
\text{(S2)} \quad & G_y\,\alpha\,\kappa_v + G_v\,\beta\,\gamma \leq 0, \\
\text{(S3)} \quad & (\sigma_q, \gamma) \in \mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}} \text{ AND } A_2 > 0.
\end{align*}
Then all three Routh--Hurwitz inequalities hold strictly for every $\kappa \geq 0$:
$c_2(\kappa) > 0$, $c_0(\kappa) > 0$, $H(\kappa) > 0$. Consequently the
equilibrium is asymptotically stable on the entire physical half-line
$\kappa \in [0, \infty)$ — no codim-1 bifurcation (Hopf, saddle-node, or
trace flip) occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$c_2(\kappa) = -\kappa G_y + (\alpha + \kappa_v)$. Under (S1) the first term
is $\geq 0$ for $\kappa \geq 0$, and $\alpha + \kappa_v > 0$, so $c_2 > 0$
strictly.

$c_0(\kappa) = -\kappa(G_y \alpha \kappa_v + G_v \beta \gamma) + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\gamma$.
Under (S2) the linear-in-$\kappa$ slope is $\geq 0$, so $c_0(\kappa) \geq c_0(0) = \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\gamma > 0$
strictly (the model assumes $\beta\gamma > 0$).

$H(\kappa) = A_2 \kappa^2 + A_1 \kappa + A_0$. Under (S3) the wedge definition
\eqref{eq:no-hopf-wedge} rules out any positive root, and $A_2 > 0$ ensures the
parabola opens upward. The only remaining route to $H(\kappa) \leq 0$ on
$\kappa > 0$ would be $H(0) = A_0 \leq 0$, contradicting the wedge classifier
which is constructed to admit only $A_0 > 0$ configurations.

All three strict inequalities together give Liu's full Routh--Hurwitz criterion
\citep{liu1994}; the spectral abscissa $\max_i \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_i(J(\kappa))$
stays strictly negative for every $\kappa \geq 0$, so no codim-1 bifurcation
occurs and the equilibrium is asymptotically stable globally on the
$\kappa$-half-line.
\end{proof}

## Numerical scan

\Cref{fig:saddle-node-wedge} renders the verdict on a $41 \times 41$ grid over
$(\sigma_q, \gamma) \in [0.02, 0.40] \times [0.05, 5.0]$ at the canonical
specification, with $\kappa \in [0, 100]$ sampled on $80$ points per cell. Of
$1{,}681$ cells, $123$ fall inside $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$
(all in the small-$\gamma$ baseline-stable corner). Every one of the $123$ wedge
cells satisfies (S1)+(S2)+(S3), and the empirical maximum of
$\max_i \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_i(J(\kappa))$ over the wedge $\times$ $\kappa$-scan is
$-6.6 \times 10^{-3}$ — strictly negative, consistent with the closed-form
verdict. No positive saddle-node $\kappa_{\mathrm{SN}}$ is detected at any wedge
cell, completing the §3.7 BT-empty result. Run dir:
\texttt{runs/saddle\_node\_wedge/}.

## Economic interpretation

\Cref{thm:no-hopf-wedge} closes the codim-1 bifurcation taxonomy of the
canonical reflexive skeleton: combined with \cref{thm:hopf} (Hopf at $\kappa^\star$
where the wedge does not apply) and \cref{thm:bt-empty} (saddle-node curve
unphysical), the picture is now complete. The wedge is the parameter
region where dealer-gamma coupling at the ATM-anchored equilibrium has
$G_y \leq 0$ (gamma is locally decreasing in log-spot, a configuration generic
to the right tail of an ATM-centred OI distribution) and where the leverage flux
$\beta\gamma$ is small enough to keep the bare Heston-with-memory triangle
sub-critical at $\kappa = 0$. Inside this region, ramping the reflexive coupling
$\kappa$ never destabilises the equilibrium — the dealer-gamma channel acts as
a \emph{stabiliser} of the variance dynamics rather than a destabiliser.

The economic content is then sharp: a market parameterised inside
$\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$ is \emph{structurally cycle-free}. There is no Hopf
limit cycle to settle on, no saddle-node bifurcation to discontinuously jump
through, no trace flip to leak instability through a real eigenvalue, and no
homoclinic spike-and-recovery dynamics from a BT point. Endogenous volatility
cycles in a market sitting inside the wedge can only originate from
non-stationary parameter drift (the OI distribution centre $\mu_q$ moving on a
slow scale that brings the system into the Hopf region), exogenous shocks, or
the small-$\beta\gamma$ region of $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$ flipping sign of
$G_y$ at $a^\star \neq \mu_q$ (a non-ATM equilibrium with locally increasing
gamma). This is a sharp falsifiable prediction for Phase 4: an SPX-calibrated
$(\sigma_q^{\mathrm{SPX}}, \gamma^{\mathrm{SPX}})$ landing inside
$\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{NH}}$ rules out endogenous reflexive cycles as the
generator of empirically observed volatility clustering at that calibration.
