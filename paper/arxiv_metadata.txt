Title: Dealer-Gamma Feedback and Local Volatility Cycles: A Fixed-Equilibrium Bifurcation Model and a Pre-Extraction Identification Protocol

Abstract (plain text for the arXiv form; $...$ math is supported):

We ask whether dealer-gamma feedback can generate a local volatility cycle without moving the reference equilibrium as feedback strength varies or violating the zero-variance boundary. The physical-measure model has three states: detrended log price, instantaneous variance with square-root diffusion, and an exponentially filtered price signal. Centering the dealer-book pressure fixes the equilibrium, while making variance feedback proportional to variance leaves the boundary drift pointing inward. For a general three-times differentiable book functional, we give all Routh--Hurwitz conditions for a local Hopf bifurcation, derive the exact eigenvalue crossing speed, and evaluate the first Lyapunov coefficient that classifies the nearby cycles. For a centered Gaussian density of latent signed dealer positions in log moneyness, the Hopf equation is quadratic in the coupling. The first positive local Hopf point of a deliberately constructed, non-calibrated configuration has $\kappa^\star=31.4933\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, $\omega^\star=47.1186\,\mathrm{rad}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, and $\ell_1=-6.2888$. Integration of the actual nonlinear book gives an attracting cycle, and its local amplitude scales approximately as $\{(\kappa-\kappa^\star)/\kappa^\star\}^{0.509}$ near the threshold. Robustness checks over signed Gaussian mixtures show that both existence and cycle stability depend on the latent book: dispersed or offsetting books can make the Hopf subcritical, and reversing the canonical orientation removes every positive local Hopf root. The result is therefore a mechanism possibility, not an S&P 500 calibration. Because public open interest identifies neither participant nor side, we also fix a sign-agnostic predictive protocol for a historical horizon before proprietary-data extraction. Its target is predictive association, not dealer inventory or causality. No registered market dataset is analysed in this paper.
