Abstract (plain text for the arXiv form; $...$ math is supported):

We study a continuous-time Heston-like stochastic-volatility model in which the spot price is coupled to its own option market through a dealer-gamma feedback channel, computed from the open-interest grid, with the drift coupling motivated by Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman (2009) demand pressure. The state is three-dimensional: spot $S_t$, instantaneous variance $v_t$, and a low-pass-filtered log-spot memory variable $\chi_t$. At zero coupling it reduces to standard time-dependent Heston (Heston 1993). We work out the Hopf bifurcation of the deterministic skeleton, with first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and a Khasminskii-style stochastic shift $\Lambda$, and we pre-register the evaluation protocol before touching any data: a direct, model-free dealer-gamma (GEX) regression on the open-interest grid as the primary test, with a critical-slowing-down detector and a sliced-Wasserstein-2 surface metric, anchored by an OpenTimestamps proof. For a representative dimensionless calibration we obtain a critical coupling $\kappa^\star \approx 0.8964$, angular frequency $\omega^\star \approx 0.5724$ rad/yr, and $\ell_1 \approx -0.025 < 0$, so the bifurcation is supercritical: an attracting limit cycle in volatility is born for $\kappa > \kappa^\star$. Dropping the memory leaves a 2D system whose Jacobian is upper-triangular; it admits only a saddle-node onset and recovers the discrete-time recursion of Dai (2025), so the third state is what makes the Hopf possible. For log-normal open-interest in moneyness both $\kappa^\star$ and $\ell_1$ are closed-form, which gives a parametric phase boundary in $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$ space.
