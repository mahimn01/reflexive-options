\begin{abstract}
We study a continuous-time Heston-like stochastic-volatility model in
which the underlying spot price is coupled to its own option market
through a dealer-gamma feedback channel, derived from the open-interest
grid via a G\^arleanu--Pedersen--Poteshman \citep{garleanu2009demand}
demand-pressure mapping. The model state is three-dimensional---spot
$S_t$, instantaneous variance $v_t$, and a low-pass-filtered log-spot
memory variable $z_t$---and reduces to standard time-dependent Heston
\citep{heston1993} at zero coupling. We synthesise three previously
separate ingredients: (i) a continuous-time SV backbone with explicit
dealer-gamma drift; (ii) a formal Hopf bifurcation calculus on the
deterministic skeleton with first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and
Khasminskii-style stochastic shift $\Lambda$; and (iii) a pre-registered
sliced-Wasserstein-2 evaluation protocol for RL-trained derivatives
agents. For a representative dimensionless calibration we obtain
$\kappa^\star \approx 0.8964$, $\omega^\star \approx 0.5724$~rad/yr, and
$\ell_1 \approx -0.025 < 0$ (supercritical: an attracting limit cycle
in volatility is born for $\kappa > \kappa^\star$). The 2D reduction has
an upper-triangular Jacobian and admits only saddle-node onset,
recovering the discrete-time recursion of \citet{dai2025gamma}; the 3D
extension is what unlocks the Hopf, distinguishing this work from
\citet{halperin2025marketron}'s Marketron quasi-particle SDE. For
log-normal open-interest in moneyness we obtain a closed-form Hopf
threshold and a closed-form $\ell_1$, exposing a parametric phase
boundary in $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$ space.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Real options markets exhibit endogenous volatility cycles that pure
stochastic-volatility (SV) models cannot reproduce. The empirical anchor
is \citet{hardiman2013}, who fit Hawkes-process branching ratios to
E-mini mid-price changes over 1998--2011 and find $n \approx 1$
throughout---the precise critical edge between sub- and super-criticality
of endogenous events. \citet{soros2009reflexivity} and
\citet{bouchaud2011endo} are the conceptual antecedents;
\citet{filimonov2012} formalised the reflexivity-quantification programme
that \citet{hardiman2013} executed. We ask what continuous-time mechanism
produces this critical reflexivity, and how the analytical structure that
quantifies it should be evaluated against historical surface dynamics.

We contribute the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item A 3D reflexive SV simulator $(S_t, v_t, z_t)$ with explicit
dealer-gamma feedback derived from the option open-interest grid via the
\citet{garleanu2009demand} demand-pressure mapping. The variance backbone
is Heston \citep{heston1993}; the memory equation
$\dot z = -\alpha z + \beta \log(S/S_0)$ closes the leverage feedback
channel that the 2D reduction lacks.
\item A Hopf bifurcation theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:hopf}) for the
deterministic skeleton at $\kappa^\star$ given by Liu's criterion, with
closed-form first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and closed-form Hopf
threshold for log-normal open-interest in moneyness
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:lognorm-kappastar}--\ref{eq:lognorm-ell1}).
\item A numerical phase diagram in $(\xi, \rho, \sigma_v)$ together with
the Khasminskii-style stochastic-Hopf shift $\Lambda$ and a parametric
phase boundary in $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$ space.
\item A pre-registration of the empirical evaluation pipeline
(sliced-$W_2$ over 21-day surface windows, $\kappa$-sensitivity slope,
Hopf-frequency spectral test) at a verifiable git commit hash with an
OpenTimestamps Bitcoin-anchored proof, in the spirit of
\citet{camerer2018} adapted for computational finance.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Closest precedents.} \citet{halperin2025marketron} (Marketron)
share the model-structural axis but do not perform the bifurcation
analysis. \citet{dai2025gamma} share the dealer-gamma axis but produce a
saddle-node onset. \citet{hesuttergonon2025} (NeurIPS 2025) share the
RL-with-environmental-sweep idea but train separate agents per grid point
rather than reporting the slope at an anchor. The continuous-time HAM-Hopf
precedents \citep{heli2009,chiarellahehommes2006} use delay-differential
heterogeneous-belief models without an options-grid feedback channel;
\citet{brockhommeswagener2009}'s ``more hedging instruments may
destabilize markets'' result is the methodological template for
derivative-driven Neimark--Sacker bifurcations. The hedger-flow-feedback
foundations are
\citet{freystremme1997,sircarpapanicolaou1998,platenschweizer1998}.

\section{The reflexive model}
\label{sec:model}

Let $S_t$ be the underlying spot price, $v_t$ the instantaneous variance,
and $z_t$ a memory variable encoding low-pass-filtered price history.
The reflexive system is
\begin{align}
\frac{dS_t}{S_t} &= \bigl(\mu + \kappa\, G(S_t, z_t, v_t)\bigr)\, dt + \sigma(S_t, v_t)\, dW^S_t, \label{eq:reflexive-S}\\
dv_t &= \bigl(\kappa_v(\theta_v - v_t) + \gamma\, z_t\bigr)\, dt + \xi \sqrt{v_t}\, dW^v_t, \label{eq:reflexive-v}\\
dz_t &= \bigl(-\alpha\, z_t + \beta\, \log(S_t / S_0)\bigr)\, dt, \label{eq:reflexive-z}
\end{align}
with $d\langle W^S, W^v\rangle = \rho\, dt$, reflexive coupling
$\kappa \geq 0$, leverage strength $\gamma \geq 0$, memory decay
$\alpha > 0$, $\beta$ dimensionless, and $G$ the aggregate dealer-gamma
exposure derived from the OI grid:
\begin{equation}
G(S, t) \;=\; \sum_{K, T} q_{K, T}\,\Gamma_{K, T}(S, t)\,\mathrm{sign}(K, T),
\label{eq:G-aggregator}
\end{equation}
following the SqueezeMetrics dealer-net convention
\citep{squeezemetrics2017}. When $\kappa = \gamma = 0$ the system collapses
to standard Heston \citep{heston1993} (verified numerically). For variance
we use the full-truncation Euler scheme of \citet{lordkoekkoekvandijk2010}.

\paragraph{Why three states.} A 2D specification (drop $z_t$ and the
$\gamma z$ term) has a Jacobian
$J_{2D}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a(\kappa)&b(\kappa)\\0&-\kappa_v\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
upper triangular with real eigenvalues: only saddle-node /
transcritical bifurcations are admissible. To Hopf, the feedback must flow
in both directions; the leverage channel $\gamma z$ together with the
auxiliary memory equation \eqref{eq:reflexive-z} is the minimal structural
modification that supplies it---structurally analogous to the memory
variable in Marketron \citep{halperin2025marketron} though with very
different mechanistic content.

\section{Hopf bifurcation theorem and closed-form \texorpdfstring{$\ell_1$}{ell-1}}
\label{sec:hopf}

\subsection{Equilibria, Jacobian, Liu criterion}
\label{sec:hopf-jac}

Drop the Brownian terms and work in log-deviation variables
$(y, u, z) = (\log(S/S^\star), v - \theta_v, z - z^\star)$ around the
equilibrium $(S^\star, \theta_v, z^\star)$ defined by
\begin{equation}
\mu - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2(S^\star, \theta_v) + \kappa\, G(S^\star, z^\star, \theta_v) = 0, \quad z^\star = \tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\log(S^\star/S_0).
\end{equation}
The deterministic skeleton becomes $\dot{\bm{x}} = J(\kappa)\bm{x}$ with
\begin{equation}
J(\kappa) = \begin{pmatrix}
a(\kappa) & b(\kappa) & \kappa\, G_z \\
0 & -\kappa_v & \gamma \\
\beta & 0 & -\alpha
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
where $a(\kappa) = \kappa G_x - \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_x\sigma^2$,
$b(\kappa) = \kappa G_v - \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_v\sigma^2$. The
characteristic polynomial is
$P(\lambda;\kappa) = \lambda^3 + c_2\lambda^2 + c_1\lambda + c_0$ with
$c_2 = -a + \kappa_v + \alpha$, $c_1 = -a\kappa_v - a\alpha + \kappa_v\alpha
- \kappa G_z\beta$, $c_0 = -a\kappa_v\alpha - \beta(\kappa G_z\kappa_v + b\gamma)$.
A 3D system Hopfs iff (Liu's criterion):
\begin{equation}
c_2 > 0,\ c_0 > 0,\ H(\kappa) := c_1 c_2 - c_0 = 0,\ H'(\kappa) \neq 0.
\label{eq:hopf-condition}
\end{equation}
The Hopf threshold is
$\kappa^\star = \{\kappa>0: H(\kappa) = 0,\ H'(\kappa)\neq 0,\ c_2,c_0 >0\}$.
At $\kappa^\star$, $J$ has imaginary pair
$\lambda_\pm = \pm i\omega^\star = \pm i\sqrt{c_1(\kappa^\star)}$ plus
real eigenvalue $\lambda_3 = -c_2(\kappa^\star) < 0$.

\subsection{Theorem 1}
\label{sec:hopf-thm}

\begin{theorem}[Hopf bifurcation in the gamma-coupled SV skeleton]
\label{thm:hopf}
Let the deterministic system (\ref{eq:reflexive-S}--\ref{eq:reflexive-z})
satisfy:
(A1) $G,\sigma^2 \in C^3$ with $\sigma^2$ bounded below by a positive
constant;
(A2) a locally unique equilibrium for every
$\kappa \in [0, \kappa_{\max}]$ with non-degenerate linearisation
(multiple equilibria may exist globally; analysis is local);
(A3) $\kappa \mapsto (a(\kappa), G_z, G_v)$ in $C^3$;
(A4) there exists $\kappa^\star \in (0, \kappa_{\max})$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:hopf-condition} with $\omega^\star := \sqrt{c_1(\kappa^\star)} > 0$;
(A5) $\ell_1(\kappa^\star) \neq 0$ \citep[eq.~3.20]{kuznetsov2004}.
Then the equilibrium undergoes a Hopf bifurcation at $\kappa = \kappa^\star$:
(1) for $\kappa$ in a one-sided neighbourhood of $\kappa^\star$ there is a
unique smooth family of periodic orbits $\Gamma_\kappa$ of period
$T_\kappa = 2\pi/\omega^\star + O(\kappa-\kappa^\star)$ and amplitude
$\propto \sqrt{|\kappa-\kappa^\star|}$;
(2) orbits are stable iff $\ell_1(\kappa^\star) < 0$ (supercritical);
(3) on $\Gamma_\kappa$, $u_t = v_t-\theta_v$ oscillates with amplitude
$\propto \sqrt{|\kappa-\kappa^\star|}$, providing endogenous volatility
cycles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The eigenvalue pair $\lambda_\pm(\kappa)$ is smooth near $\kappa^\star$
by the implicit function theorem on $P$. Routh--Hurwitz gives
$\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_\pm(\kappa^\star) = 0$, and $H'(\kappa^\star) \neq 0$
translates to $\frac{d}{d\kappa}\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_\pm \neq 0$. The
centre manifold theorem \citep[Thm.~5.4]{kuznetsov2004} gives a 2D
invariant manifold tangent to the eigenspace of $\pm i\omega^\star$;
conjugation to Poincar\'e normal form and \citet[Thm.~3.3]{kuznetsov2004}
yield (1)--(3).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical anchor.} For
$\{G_x, G_v, G_z\}{=}\{0.5,{-}0.5,{-}0.5\}$, $\alpha{=}0.5$/yr,
$\beta{=}1$, $\gamma{=}0.5$, $\kappa_v{=}2$/yr (constant-vol surrogate):
$\kappa^\star {=} 0.8964$, $\omega^\star {=} 0.5724$~rad/yr,
$\lambda_3 {=} -2.052$, $\ell_1 {=} -2.53\times 10^{-2}$ (supercritical).
Integrating the deterministic skeleton at $\kappa = 1.05\,\kappa^\star$
from random initial conditions, the trajectory converges to a closed
orbit whose measured period $T_{\mathrm{measured}} = 10.561$~yr matches
the linear prediction $2\pi/\omega^\star = 10.977$~yr to 3.79\%---within
the leading-order $\mathcal{O}(\kappa-\kappa^\star)$ Hopf-normal-form
correction.

\subsection{Closed-form \texorpdfstring{$\kappa^\star$ and $\ell_1$}{kappa-star and ell-1} for log-normal OI}
\label{sec:hopf-closed-form}

For log-normal open-interest in log-strike,
$q(\log K) \propto \exp(-(\log K - \mu_q)^2/(2\sigma_q^2))$, the
Gaussian-product identity collapses the OI integral to
\begin{equation}
G(a, v) \;=\; \mathcal{C}(v)\cdot e^{-a}\cdot \exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{(a - m(v))^2}{2\,\tau^2(v)}\bigr),
\label{eq:G-closed-form}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{C}(v) = \kappa_u e^{-q_{\mathrm{div}}T_{\mathrm{eff}}}/\sqrt{2\pi\tau^2(v)}$,
$\tau^2(v) = \sigma_q^2 + v\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}$,
$m(v) = \mu_q - (r - q_{\mathrm{div}} + \tfrac{1}{2}v)T_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
With $G_z = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = v$, $H(\kappa) = c_1 c_2 - c_0$
degree-collapses from cubic to quadratic; writing $A = \alpha + \kappa_v$,
$M = \alpha\kappa_v$, $L = \beta\gamma$, the Hopf threshold is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\kappa^\star = \frac{G_y A^2 - G_v L \;-\; \sqrt{D}}{2\,G_y^2\, A},\;}
\quad D := (G_v L - G_y A^2)^2 - 4 G_y^2 A\bigl(MA - \tfrac{L}{2}\bigr).
\label{eq:lognorm-kappastar}
\end{equation}
The discriminant $D < 0$ implies no Hopf at any $\kappa$ and
unconditional stability of the deterministic skeleton.

The third-order partials of \eqref{eq:G-closed-form} are explicit rational
functions of $(\delta := a^\star - m(v^\star), \tau^2, T_{\mathrm{eff}})$,
so the bilinear and trilinear tensors $B$, $C$ at $\kappa^\star$ admit a
closed form. Substituting into the Kuznetsov formula
\begin{multline}
\ell_1(\kappa^\star) = \tfrac{1}{2\omega^\star}\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[\langle p, C(q,q,\bar q)\rangle \\
- 2\langle p, B(q, J^{-1}B(q,\bar q))\rangle \\
+ \langle p, B(\bar q, (2i\omega^\star I - J)^{-1}B(q,q))\rangle\bigr],
\label{eq:lognorm-ell1}
\end{multline}
with $Jq = i\omega^\star q$, $J^\top p = -i\omega^\star p$,
$\langle p, q\rangle = 1$, yields a $\sim\!30$-term closed-form rational
in the $G$-partials and $(\kappa_v, \alpha, \beta, \gamma)$, evaluated
in $\sim\!50\,\mu$s per parameter tuple and matching the FD-tensor
pipeline to $<0.6\%$. At the canonical regime ($\sigma_q{=}0.10$,
$T_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}0.25$, $\kappa_v{=}2$, $\alpha{=}0.05$, $\beta{=}1$,
$\gamma{=}1$, $\mu_q{=}\log 100$, $v^\star{=}0.04$):
$\kappa^\star {=} 17.81$, $\omega^\star {=} 1.18$~rad/yr,
$\ell_1 {=} -4.81 \times 10^{-1}$ (supercritical). With the closed form
in hand, the sign of $\ell_1$ near the boundary contour is structurally
certain rather than statistically inferred.

\section{Numerical phase diagram}
\label{sec:phase}

The full $(\kappa, \xi, \rho, \sigma_v)$ phase diagram is computed by
sweeping $\kappa \in [0, 2]$ on a 401-point grid for each of
$31 \times 21 \times 4 = 2{,}604$ cells over $(\xi, \rho, \sigma_v)$ at
the canonical Hopf-exhibiting regime; the pair $(\xi, \rho)$ enters via
$a_{\mathrm{eff}}(\kappa) = a(\kappa) + \tfrac{1}{2}\xi^2\rho G_v$, an
\citet{engellambrasmussen2024}-style projection of the small-noise
stochastic Hopf onto the eigenvalue envelope. Wall-clock $\sim\!16$s on a
single M-series core.

The SPX-relevant $(\xi, \rho) \approx (0.30, -0.70)$ corner has
$\kappa^\star$ in the $0.85$--$0.95$ band, sitting \emph{near but not
across} the Hopf boundary---the structural mechanism we conjecture for
the empirical $n \approx 1$ branching ratio of \citet{hardiman2013}.
Khasminskii sphere-process estimates \citep{khasminskii1980,arnold1998}
on a $6 \times 6$ $(\xi, \rho)$ grid give
$|\Lambda(\kappa^\star)| \in [5\!\times\!10^{-2}, 9\!\times\!10^{-2}]$
with empirical scaling exponent $\hat B = 0.082$ (95\% CI
$[-0.010, 0.168]$); the \citet{engellambrasmussen2024} prediction $B = 2/3$
is rejected at $p \ll 0.01$ at this trivial-$G$ equilibrium where ELR's
shear-stretch term is identically zero. Operationally,
$|\Lambda|\cdot\varepsilon^2 \ll 1$ at calibration-noise scale: the
deterministic $\kappa^\star$ is the relevant threshold.

\section{Pre-registered evaluation framework}
\label{sec:eval}

The pipeline is committed at git commit \texttt{[anonymized]} with an
OpenTimestamps Bitcoin-anchored proof of the document hash, prior to any
contact with empirical SPX data. This addresses the documented
computational-reproducibility crisis in finance \citep{perignon2024repro}
and adapts the \citet{camerer2018} pre-registration template to a
computational-finance / RL-for-derivatives context. We do not claim
``first pre-registration in finance''---the Pacific-Basin Finance Journal
pathway \citep{faff2022prereg} and the AEA RCT Registry \citep{aearct}
predate this work. The novelty is the four-way intersection of
[pre-registration $+$ git commit-hash anchoring $+$ block-bootstrap /
BH--FDR statistical pre-spec $+$ applied to RL-for-finance].

\paragraph{Two evaluation primitives.}
(i) Sliced-Wasserstein-2 \citep{bonneel2015sliced} over arbitrage-free
21-day surface windows---the realism metric.
(ii) $\kappa$-sensitivity slope at calibrated $\kappa_0$---the
reflexivity-importance scalar.

\paragraph{H1 (primary realism).} A model-free RL agent $\pi_{\kappa_0}$
trained inside the reflexive simulator at $\kappa_0$ produces an
IV-surface distribution closer in sliced-$W_2$ over arbitrage-free
21-trading-day rolling windows to the empirical SPX surface than four
non-reflexive baselines (time-dep Heston, LSV, 3/2 SV,
gamma-aware-non-reflexive). Three event windows: Volmageddon
(2018-02-05), COVID (2020-03-16), 2024 yen carry unwind (2024-08-05).
Decision rule: 12 pairwise dominance checks (4 baselines $\times$ 3
windows) with non-overlapping 95\% block-bootstrap CIs
\citep{politisromano1994}. The arbitrage filter follows
\citet{roper2010,gatheraljacquier2014} on butterfly / calendar-spread /
Lee-bound \citep{lee2004} criteria; sliced-$W_2$ sample-complexity follows
\citet{manole2022sw}.

\paragraph{Sliced-$W_2$ sample-complexity audit.} A pre-data audit
characterises the bootstrap-95\%-CI half-width on sliced-$W_2$ between
two Heston populations differing only in their long-run variance
($\theta_A = 0.04$ vs $\theta_B = 0.0576$). Each side draws from a
$5{,}000$-day population; the ``true'' SW2 averaged over $10$
slice-direction MC repeats on the full populations equals $0.0555$.
For $n_{\mathrm{windows}} \in \{30, 100, 300, 1{,}000, 3{,}000\}$ we
sub-sample, compute the SW2 estimate, and report the median
bootstrap-CI half-width across $30$ outer seeds (\cref{tab:sw2-sample-complexity}).

\paragraph{H2--H4 (secondary).} H2 is the $\kappa$-sensitivity slope
$\tilde\rho = \partial\hat m/\partial\kappa\rvert_{\kappa_0}$---positive
and statistically distinguishable from zero for the reflexive-trained
agent, statistically indistinguishable for baselines (TOST equivalence
on the dimensionless elasticity per A7, with the GP slope CI under the
pre-registered RBF + WhiteKernel kernel of A6 and elliptic uncertainty
sets \citep{ma2025robust}). H3 is post-FOMC RR25 and ATM-term-structure
shifts. H4 is the Hopf-frequency Welch-PSD peak in $|r_t|$ at
$\omega^\star \pm 20\%$ vs an IAAFT \citep{schreiberschmitz1996} surrogate
null. IAAFT calibration on Heston and AR(1) $H_0$ generators bounds the
empirical FPR at $\leq 7\%$ at $\alpha = 0.05$; on Stuart--Landau positive
controls the detector achieves $\geq 80\%$ power at $T \geq 512$ daily
samples for $8/9$ $(\mu, \sigma)$ configurations. All H1--H4 use BH--FDR
across the joint family.

\paragraph{Differentiation from precedents.} \citet{ning2024arbitrage}
use $W_1$ on single-day surface marginals; we use sliced-$W_2$ on 21-day
windows with a hard arbitrage pre-filter, block-bootstrap CIs, and
per-regime reporting. \citet{hesuttergonon2025} sweep a Wasserstein-ball
perturbation budget with a separate adversarially-trained agent at each
grid point; we deploy a single agent across the $\kappa$ grid and report
the slope at anchor with a GP-posterior CI \citep{ma2025robust} for the
elliptic-uncertainty-set analogue. \citet{subbaswamy2022} (NeurIPS 2022)
provide the closest theoretical precursor for slope-at-anchor robustness
summaries; we operationalise it empirically and apply it to RL-for-finance
\citep{bai2024rlfinance,huang2024openrlbench}.

\subsection{Synthetic pipeline validation}
\label{sec:eval-synthetic-validation}

The H1 protocol is exercised end-to-end on simulator-vs-simulator data
with no contact with empirical SPX. Three sources, each $n = 30$
trajectories at the production budget: (a) the trained agent acts inside
the SAME reflexive simulator at $\kappa_0$ (different seeds; expected
indistinguishable from reference); (b) the agent at $4\cdot\kappa_0$
(four-fold synthetic-anchor coupling change; expected measurably further);
(c) Heston with no dealer-gamma coupling and a more aggressive
$(\rho, \xi) = (-0.70, 0.30)$ backbone (different mechanism; expected
furthest).

\section{Mechanism decomposition}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The reflexive simulator and the Marketron quasi-particle SDE
\citep{halperin2025marketron} are not the same mechanism: in our model
the memory carrier is the low-pass-filtered log-price $z$ entering only
the variance drift via $\gamma z$; in Marketron it is the unobservable $y$
entering both drifts non-linearly through a potential $V_M(x)$. Even at
zero coupling our model collapses to standard Heston while Marketron does
not. We instead report mechanism decomposition: a 5D coarse-grid tuning
over $(\kappa,\gamma,T_{\mathrm{eff}},\mu_q,\sigma_q)$, then per-cell
classification into \texttt{shape\_target} / \texttt{level\_artifact} /
\texttt{calibration\_artifact}.

The headline shape-feature match rate is $8/24$ (33\%, one-sided binomial
$p \approx 0.27$, $\geq 30\%$ gate passed), with long-horizon
($\geq 0.5$~y) excess-kurt sign agreement on $5/6$ horizons. The
skew-sign disagreement is predictable: under risk-neutral drift our
long-horizon skew is dealer-gamma-plus-leverage-driven, while Marketron's
positive long-horizon skew comes from \citet{bessembinder2018}
compounding under calibrated drift. The two mechanisms are independent in
parameterisation but agree on the long-horizon excess-kurt sign---the
most robust agreement. An a-priori-restricted subset
(\texttt{is\_mechanism\_relevant\_cell}, locked before per-cell outcomes
were inspected: long-horizon, shape-only, within-envelope) gives $4/8$
(50\%, $p = 0.637$) at the out-of-sample budget, driven primarily by
Table~5 long-horizon skew flipping more strongly negative; the strengthened
restriction does not reach $p < 0.05$ at the present per-set tuned-coupling
sample size and the empirical Phase~4 evaluation will be needed to
adjudicate.

\section{Conclusions and future work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have introduced a 3D reflexive SV simulator $(S_t, v_t, z_t)$ with
explicit dealer-gamma drift, proved a Hopf bifurcation theorem with
closed-form first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and Hopf threshold
$\kappa^\star$ for log-normal open-interest in moneyness, computed a
numerical $(\xi,\rho,\sigma_v)$ phase diagram and a $(\sigma_q,\gamma)$
closed-form phase boundary, and pre-registered the empirical evaluation
pipeline (sliced-$W_2$, $\kappa$-sensitivity slope, Hopf-frequency
spectral test) at a verifiable git commit hash with an OpenTimestamps
Bitcoin-anchored proof. The synthetic-pipeline validation demonstrates
the locked H1 protocol returns the predicted ordering on data with known
underlying physics. The empirical SPX calibration is the natural Phase~4
follow-up, gated on data acquisition; results---confirming or refuting
H1--H4---are pre-committed to publication regardless of direction. Open
theoretical items: closed-form $\Lambda(\kappa)$ via
Engel--Lamb--Rasmussen-style asymptotics adapted to Heston multiplicative
noise (currently numerical only); a formal Hawkes-$n$ to
SV-Jacobian-eigenvalue reduction relating $\kappa^\star$ to the empirical
$n \approx 1$ \citep{hardiman2013,bondi2024hawkes,bacrymastromatteomuzy2015}.
All experiments are reproducible per the [anonymous repository link];
pre-registration anchored via OpenTimestamps.
