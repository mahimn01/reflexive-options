\begin{abstract}
We give a local physical-measure model in which detrended log price,
square-root variance, and a filtered price signal form a feedback loop. The
reference equilibrium is fixed as coupling varies and variance drift points
inward at zero. For a centered Gaussian density of signed dealer positions,
the Routh--Hurwitz determinant is quadratic in coupling. An illustrative,
non-calibrated configuration has $\kappa^\star=31.4933$ yr$^{-1}$,
$\omega^\star=47.1186$ rad yr$^{-1}$, and $\ell_1=-6.2888$; integrating the
actual nonlinear book gives a nearby attracting cycle with positive variance.
The result is local and deterministic. Public open interest does not identify
dealer sign, so a future WRDS study is pre-specified as a registered-horizon
predictive proxy analysis rather than a structural confirmation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and scope}

Option delta hedging creates state-dependent demand. Long-gamma rebalancing is
locally contrarian and short-gamma rebalancing is locally trend-following
\citep{freystremme1997,sircarpapanicolaou1998,platenschweizer1998}. Demand-based
option pricing shows that intermediary constraints affect option prices
\citep{garleanu2009demand}; it does not derive a spot drift. We therefore treat
the feedback below as a reduced-form physical-measure hypothesis.

Three safeguards distinguish the model from its earlier draft. First, a
centered book functional pins one equilibrium for every coupling. Second, the
memory term in variance is multiplied by variance, preserving inward boundary
drift. Third, theory uses latent signed dealer positions, while the empirical
protocol treats public open interest as unsigned. Open interest counts
outstanding contracts and does not identify participant or side
\citep{oicopeninterest}; participant-tagged data are needed to estimate dealer
positions \citep{vasquez2025zerodte}.

\section{Centered physical-measure model}

Let $X_t=\log(S_t/F_t)$ for a predictable reference $F_t$ that absorbs
baseline expected return. Locally,
\begin{align}
dX_t&=[-\delta X_t-\tfrac12(v_t-\theta_v)+\kappa g(X_t,\chi_t,v_t)]dt
       +\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^S,\label{eq:x}\\
dv_t&=[\kappa_v(\theta_v-v_t)+\gamma v_t\chi_t]dt
       +\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^v,\label{eq:v}\\
d\chi_t&=\alpha(\beta X_t-\chi_t)dt,
\qquad d\langle W^S,W^v\rangle_t=\rho dt.\label{eq:chi}
\end{align}
All dynamics are under $\mathbb P$. At $v=0$, the variance drift is
$\kappa_v\theta_v>0$ and diffusion is zero. This is boundary-compatible; no
global non-explosion theorem is claimed.

For fixed log moneyness, a Gaussian positive book mass integrates against
Black--Scholes gamma as
\begin{align}
\mathcal G(X,v)&=\frac{e^{-q_dT-X}}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau^2(v)}}
\exp[-(X-m(v))^2/(2\tau^2(v))],\nonumber\\
\tau^2(v)&=\sigma_q^2+vT,\qquad
m(v)=\mu_q-(r-q_d+v/2)T.\label{eq:raw}
\end{align}
We use
\begin{equation}
g(X,\chi,v)=s\frac{\mathcal G(X,v)-\mathcal G(0,\theta_v)}
{\mathcal G(0,\theta_v)},\qquad s\in\{-1,+1\}.\label{eq:centered}
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is a model input for signed dealer orientation, not an OI statistic.
Centering gives the fixed equilibrium $z_\star=(0,\theta_v,0)$ for every
$\kappa$.

\section{Local Hopf criterion}

At $z_\star$, write $G_X=g_X$, $G_v=g_v$, $G_\chi=g_\chi$. The Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
J=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&d\\0&-\kappa_v&c\\m&0&-\alpha\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
Here $a=-\delta+\kappa G_X$, $b=-1/2+\kappa G_v$,
$d=\kappa G_\chi$, $c=\gamma\theta_v$, and $m=\alpha\beta$.
For $\det(\lambda I-J)=\lambda^3+c_2\lambda^2+c_1\lambda+c_0$,
\begin{align}
c_2&=\kappa_v+\alpha-a,\nonumber\\
c_1&=\kappa_v\alpha-a(\kappa_v+\alpha)-dm,\label{eq:cs}\\
c_0&=-a\kappa_v\alpha-bcm-dm\kappa_v.\nonumber
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-equilibrium local Hopf]
Suppose the drift is $C^3$. If at $\kappa^\star>0$,
$c_2,c_1,c_0>0$, $H:=c_1c_2-c_0=0$, $H'\ne0$, and the first
Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1\ne0$, then $z_\star$ undergoes a non-degenerate
local Hopf bifurcation. The pair is $\pm i\sqrt{c_1}$, the third eigenvalue is
$-c_2$, and
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{d\operatorname{Re}\lambda_+}{d\kappa}\right|_{\kappa^\star}
=-\frac{H'(\kappa^\star)}{2(c_1+c_2^2)}.\label{eq:cross}
\end{equation}
In the convention of \citet{kuznetsov2004}, $\ell_1<0$ gives stable small
cycles on the side where the pair is unstable.
\end{theorem}

The proof factors the cubic as $(\lambda+c_2)(\lambda^2+c_1)$ at $H=0$,
derives \eqref{eq:cross} by implicit differentiation, and applies the classical
Hopf theorem. It is local: no root does not imply global stability, and a
subcritical Hopf alone does not imply hysteresis.

For the static book \eqref{eq:centered}, $G_\chi=0$. Put
$A=\alpha+\kappa_v$, $M=\alpha\kappa_v$, and
$L=(\gamma\theta_v)(\alpha\beta)$. Then
\begin{equation}
H(\kappa)=G_X^2A\kappa^2+\{G_vL-G_XA(A+2\delta)\}\kappa
+A(M+\delta A+\delta^2)-L/2.\label{eq:quadratic}
\end{equation}
Both roots are enumerated and every positivity and transversality condition is
checked. Analytic derivatives of \eqref{eq:raw} through order three enter the
standard Kuznetsov multilinear formula. The tensor from the variance equation
also includes $B_{v,v\chi}=B_{v,\chi v}=\gamma$.

\section{Nonlinear validation}

The illustrative parameters are
\[
(\delta,\kappa_v,\theta_v,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
=(0.5,40,0.0625,50,2,500)
\]
and $(\mu_q,\sigma_q,T,s)=(0.06,0.20,0.25,+1)$. These are not SPX estimates.
They give $G_X=-0.0617978$, $G_v=-2.0198878$ and
\[
\kappa^\star=31.4932976,\quad \omega^\star=47.1185670,\quad
\ell_1=-6.2888041,
\]
with crossing speed $0.2686294$. The linear period is 0.13335 years.
The same quadratic has a remote second valid point at
$\kappa^{\star\star}=16860.8961$, where the pair crosses back into the stable
half-plane; all figures labelled as thresholds use the first root.

Direct integration of the actual Gaussian functional, not a separately chosen
cubic normal form, gives
$X\in[-0.02814,0.02358]$ and $v\in[0.04453,0.08187]$ after transient
discard. Thus the displayed cycle remains in the local and positive-variance
region.
The full paper's gross-normalized mixture audit is less uniform: nearby
same-sign mixtures remain supercritical, but dispersed or offsetting books can
be subcritical. The attracting canonical cycle is therefore not a universal
book-shape prediction.

\section{Pre-extraction identification protocol}

No registered market dataset is analysed. Before expected September 2026 WRDS
access, Amendments A13--A15 fix a registered historical horizon from 2017-01-03
through 2024-10-29. They
construct four observable OI-gamma book summaries: log unsigned mass,
call--put composition, gamma-weighted mean log moneyness, and log dispersion.
Contracts are unadjusted standard-settlement SPX/SPXW with 1--365 calendar-day
maturities, fixed quote/IV/moneyness filters, and parity forwards split by
AM/PM settlement.
For each, a separate forward regression predicts the next-session log squared
return from the day-$t$ summary. Leads and HAR-style lags
remain on the complete CRSP market calendar; weekday indicators describe the
outcome session, and one monthly-expiration control describes session $t$. HAC lag
5 and a 10-day moving-block bootstrap are each BH-adjusted across four tests.
A label is robust only if both adjusted $p$-values reject and the 95\%
bootstrap interval excludes zero; one-family rejection is method-sensitive.

Stress-window interactions and convention-signed GEX are secondary. A
convention-dependent result is labelled non-identified. Even robust prediction
is association, not causation or dealer-sign identification. Participant-tagged
positions and an identification strategy are required for a structural test.
Because the historical outcomes and named episodes were already public, this
is a retrospective pre-analysis plan, not a blinded prospective experiment.

\section{Conclusion}

A carefully centered, boundary-compatible three-state model can exhibit a
non-degenerate local Hopf cycle. This is a possibility result, not a claim that
SPX is near the threshold. The central empirical limitation is categorical:
public OI is not dealer inventory. The data study is designed to stop at that
boundary rather than promote a proxy correlation into mechanism confirmation.
