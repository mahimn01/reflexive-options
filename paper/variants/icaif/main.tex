\begin{abstract}
We study a continuous-time Heston-like stochastic-volatility model in
which the underlying spot price is coupled to its own option market
through a dealer-gamma feedback channel, derived from the open-interest
grid via a G\^arleanu--Pedersen--Poteshman \citep{garleanu2009demand}
demand-pressure mapping. The model state is three-dimensional---spot
$S_t$, instantaneous variance $v_t$, and a low-pass-filtered log-spot
memory variable $z_t$---and reduces to standard time-dependent Heston
\citep{heston1993} at zero coupling. We synthesise three previously
separate ingredients: (i) a continuous-time SV backbone with explicit
dealer-gamma drift; (ii) a formal Hopf bifurcation calculus on the
deterministic skeleton with first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and
Khasminskii-style stochastic shift $\Lambda$; and (iii) a pre-registered
evaluation protocol whose primary test is a direct, model-free
dealer-gamma (GEX) regression, complemented by a sliced-Wasserstein-2
surface metric and a critical-slowing-down detector. For a representative dimensionless calibration we obtain
$\kappa^\star \approx 0.8964$, $\omega^\star \approx 0.5724$~rad/yr, and
$\ell_1 \approx -0.025 < 0$ (supercritical: an attracting limit cycle
in volatility is born for $\kappa > \kappa^\star$). The 2D reduction has
an upper-triangular Jacobian and admits only saddle-node onset,
recovering the discrete-time recursion of \citet{dai2025gamma}; the 3D
extension is what unlocks the Hopf, distinguishing this work from
\citet{halperin2025marketron}'s Marketron quasi-particle SDE. For
log-normal open-interest in moneyness we obtain a closed-form Hopf
threshold and a closed-form $\ell_1$, exposing a parametric phase
boundary in $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$ space. We additionally close three
theoretical loose ends: (i) a codim-2 Bautin/Bogdanov--Takens analysis
showing the BT locus is empty in the canonical scan window
(\cref{thm:bt-empty}); (ii) the McKean--Vlasov mean-field
correction to the single-dealer threshold, regime-dependent in sign
($\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{MV}}/\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{single}} < 1$ strictly
at the canonical short-gamma regime, with frozen-dealer limit $8/21$ and
first-order asymptote $1 - 0.664/\theta_G + O(\theta_G^{-2})$;
\cref{thm:mv-hopf}); and (iii) a Hawkes--SV criticality correspondence
placing \citet{hardiman2013}'s empirical $n \approx 1$ at the
real-eigenvalue (saddle-node) stratum and the model's Hopf as the
strictly stronger oscillatory cell beyond any scalar branching ratio
(\cref{thm:hawkes-sv}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Real options markets exhibit endogenous volatility cycles that pure
stochastic-volatility (SV) models cannot reproduce. The empirical anchor
is \citet{hardiman2013}, who fit Hawkes-process branching ratios to
E-mini mid-price changes over 1998--2011 and find $n \approx 1$
throughout---the precise critical edge between sub- and super-criticality
of endogenous events. \citet{soros2009reflexivity} and
\citet{bouchaud2011endo} are the conceptual antecedents;
\citet{filimonov2012} formalised the reflexivity-quantification programme
that \citet{hardiman2013} executed. We ask what continuous-time mechanism
produces this critical reflexivity, and how the analytical structure that
quantifies it should be evaluated against historical surface dynamics.

We contribute the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item A 3D reflexive SV simulator $(S_t, v_t, z_t)$ with explicit
dealer-gamma feedback derived from the option open-interest grid via the
\citet{garleanu2009demand} demand-pressure mapping. The variance backbone
is Heston \citep{heston1993}; the memory equation
$\dot z = -\alpha z + \beta \log(S/S_0)$ closes the leverage feedback
channel that the 2D reduction lacks.
\item A Hopf bifurcation theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:hopf}) for the
deterministic skeleton at $\kappa^\star$ given by Liu's criterion, with
closed-form first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and closed-form Hopf
threshold for log-normal open-interest in moneyness
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:lognorm-kappastar}--\ref{eq:lognorm-ell1}).
\item A numerical phase diagram in $(\xi, \rho, \sigma_v)$ together with
the Khasminskii-style stochastic-Hopf shift $\Lambda$ and a parametric
phase boundary in $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$ space.
\item A pre-registration of the empirical evaluation pipeline
(a direct dealer-gamma regression as primary test, sliced-$W_2$ over
21-day surface windows, $\kappa$-sensitivity slope, and a
critical-slowing-down detector) at a verifiable git commit hash with an
OpenTimestamps Bitcoin-anchored proof, in the spirit of
\citet{camerer2018} adapted for computational finance.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Closest precedents.} \citet{halperin2025marketron} (Marketron)
share the model-structural axis but do not perform the bifurcation
analysis. \citet{dai2025gamma} share the dealer-gamma axis but produce a
saddle-node onset. \citet{hesuttergonon2025} (NeurIPS 2025) share the
RL-with-environmental-sweep idea but train separate agents per grid point
rather than reporting the slope at an anchor. The continuous-time HAM-Hopf
precedents \citep{heli2009,chiarellahehommes2006} use delay-differential
heterogeneous-belief models without an options-grid feedback channel;
\citet{brockhommeswagener2009}'s ``more hedging instruments may
destabilize markets'' result is the methodological template for
derivative-driven Neimark--Sacker bifurcations. The hedger-flow-feedback
foundations are
\citet{freystremme1997,sircarpapanicolaou1998,platenschweizer1998}.

\section{The reflexive model}
\label{sec:model}

Let $S_t$ be the underlying spot price, $v_t$ the instantaneous variance,
and $z_t$ a memory variable encoding low-pass-filtered price history.
The reflexive system is
\begin{align}
\frac{dS_t}{S_t} &= \bigl(\mu + \kappa\, G(S_t, z_t, v_t)\bigr)\, dt + \sigma(S_t, v_t)\, dW^S_t, \label{eq:reflexive-S}\\
dv_t &= \bigl(\kappa_v(\theta_v - v_t) + \gamma\, z_t\bigr)\, dt + \xi \sqrt{v_t}\, dW^v_t, \label{eq:reflexive-v}\\
dz_t &= \bigl(-\alpha\, z_t + \beta\, \log(S_t / S_0)\bigr)\, dt, \label{eq:reflexive-z}
\end{align}
with $d\langle W^S, W^v\rangle = \rho\, dt$, reflexive coupling
$\kappa \geq 0$, leverage strength $\gamma \geq 0$, memory decay
$\alpha > 0$, $\beta$ dimensionless, and $G$ the aggregate dealer-gamma
exposure derived from the OI grid:
\begin{equation}
G(S, t) \;=\; \sum_{K, T} q_{K, T}\,\Gamma_{K, T}(S, t)\,\mathrm{sign}(K, T),
\label{eq:G-aggregator}
\end{equation}
following the SqueezeMetrics dealer-net convention
\citep{squeezemetrics2017}. When $\kappa = \gamma = 0$ the system collapses
to standard Heston \citep{heston1993} (verified numerically). For variance
we use the full-truncation Euler scheme of \citet{lordkoekkoekvandijk2010}.

\paragraph{Why three states.} A 2D specification (drop $z_t$ and the
$\gamma z$ term) has a Jacobian
$J_{2D}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a(\kappa)&b(\kappa)\\0&-\kappa_v\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
upper triangular with real eigenvalues: only saddle-node /
transcritical bifurcations are admissible. To Hopf, the feedback must flow
in both directions; the leverage channel $\gamma z$ together with the
auxiliary memory equation \eqref{eq:reflexive-z} is the minimal structural
modification that supplies it---structurally analogous to the memory
variable in Marketron \citep{halperin2025marketron} though with very
different mechanistic content.

\section{Hopf bifurcation theorem and closed-form \texorpdfstring{$\ell_1$}{ell-1}}
\label{sec:hopf}

\subsection{Equilibria, Jacobian, Liu criterion}
\label{sec:hopf-jac}

Drop the Brownian terms and work in log-deviation variables
$(y, u, z) = (\log(S/S^\star), v - \theta_v, z - z^\star)$ around the
equilibrium $(S^\star, \theta_v, z^\star)$ defined by
\begin{equation}
\mu - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2(S^\star, \theta_v) + \kappa\, G(S^\star, z^\star, \theta_v) = 0, \quad z^\star = \tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\log(S^\star/S_0).
\end{equation}
The deterministic skeleton becomes $\dot{\bm{x}} = J(\kappa)\bm{x}$ with
\begin{equation}
J(\kappa) = \begin{pmatrix}
a(\kappa) & b(\kappa) & \kappa\, G_z \\
0 & -\kappa_v & \gamma \\
\beta & 0 & -\alpha
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
where $a(\kappa) = \kappa G_x - \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_x\sigma^2$,
$b(\kappa) = \kappa G_v - \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_v\sigma^2$. The
characteristic polynomial is
$P(\lambda;\kappa) = \lambda^3 + c_2\lambda^2 + c_1\lambda + c_0$ with
$c_2 = -a + \kappa_v + \alpha$, $c_1 = -a\kappa_v - a\alpha + \kappa_v\alpha
- \kappa G_z\beta$, $c_0 = -a\kappa_v\alpha - \beta(\kappa G_z\kappa_v + b\gamma)$.
A 3D system Hopfs iff (Liu's criterion):
\begin{equation}
c_2 > 0,\ c_0 > 0,\ H(\kappa) := c_1 c_2 - c_0 = 0,\ H'(\kappa) \neq 0.
\label{eq:hopf-condition}
\end{equation}
The Hopf threshold is
$\kappa^\star = \{\kappa>0: H(\kappa) = 0,\ H'(\kappa)\neq 0,\ c_2,c_0 >0\}$.
At $\kappa^\star$, $J$ has imaginary pair
$\lambda_\pm = \pm i\omega^\star = \pm i\sqrt{c_1(\kappa^\star)}$ plus
real eigenvalue $\lambda_3 = -c_2(\kappa^\star) < 0$.

\subsection{Theorem 1}
\label{sec:hopf-thm}

\begin{theorem}[Hopf bifurcation in the gamma-coupled SV skeleton]
\label{thm:hopf}
Let the deterministic system (\ref{eq:reflexive-S}--\ref{eq:reflexive-z})
satisfy:
(A1) $G,\sigma^2 \in C^3$ with $\sigma^2$ bounded below by a positive
constant;
(A2) a locally unique equilibrium for every
$\kappa \in [0, \kappa_{\max}]$ with non-degenerate linearisation
(multiple equilibria may exist globally; analysis is local);
(A3) $\kappa \mapsto (a(\kappa), G_z, G_v)$ in $C^3$;
(A4) there exists $\kappa^\star \in (0, \kappa_{\max})$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:hopf-condition} with $\omega^\star := \sqrt{c_1(\kappa^\star)} > 0$;
(A5) $\ell_1(\kappa^\star) \neq 0$ \citep[eq.~3.20]{kuznetsov2004}.
Then the equilibrium undergoes a Hopf bifurcation at $\kappa = \kappa^\star$:
(1) for $\kappa$ in a one-sided neighbourhood of $\kappa^\star$ there is a
unique smooth family of periodic orbits $\Gamma_\kappa$ of period
$T_\kappa = 2\pi/\omega^\star + O(\kappa-\kappa^\star)$ and amplitude
$\propto \sqrt{|\kappa-\kappa^\star|}$;
(2) orbits are stable iff $\ell_1(\kappa^\star) < 0$ (supercritical);
(3) on $\Gamma_\kappa$, $u_t = v_t-\theta_v$ oscillates with amplitude
$\propto \sqrt{|\kappa-\kappa^\star|}$, providing endogenous volatility
cycles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The eigenvalue pair $\lambda_\pm(\kappa)$ is smooth near $\kappa^\star$
by the implicit function theorem on $P$. Routh--Hurwitz gives
$\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_\pm(\kappa^\star) = 0$, and $H'(\kappa^\star) \neq 0$
translates to $\frac{d}{d\kappa}\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_\pm \neq 0$. The
centre manifold theorem \citep[Thm.~5.4]{kuznetsov2004} gives a 2D
invariant manifold tangent to the eigenspace of $\pm i\omega^\star$;
conjugation to Poincar\'e normal form and \citet[Thm.~3.3]{kuznetsov2004}
yield (1)--(3).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical anchor.} For
$\{G_x, G_v, G_z\}{=}\{0.5,{-}0.5,{-}0.5\}$, $\alpha{=}0.5$/yr,
$\beta{=}1$, $\gamma{=}0.5$, $\kappa_v{=}2$/yr (constant-vol surrogate):
$\kappa^\star {=} 0.8964$, $\omega^\star {=} 0.5724$~rad/yr,
$\lambda_3 {=} -2.052$, $\ell_1 {=} -2.53\times 10^{-2}$ (supercritical).
Integrating the deterministic skeleton at $\kappa = 1.05\,\kappa^\star$
from random initial conditions, the trajectory converges to a closed
orbit whose measured period $T_{\mathrm{measured}} = 10.561$~yr matches
the linear prediction $2\pi/\omega^\star = 10.977$~yr to 3.79\%---within
the leading-order $\mathcal{O}(\kappa-\kappa^\star)$ Hopf-normal-form
correction.

\subsection{Closed-form \texorpdfstring{$\kappa^\star$ and $\ell_1$}{kappa-star and ell-1} for log-normal OI}
\label{sec:hopf-closed-form}

For log-normal open-interest in log-strike,
$q(\log K) \propto \exp(-(\log K - \mu_q)^2/(2\sigma_q^2))$, the
Gaussian-product identity collapses the OI integral to
\begin{equation}
G(a, v) \;=\; \mathcal{C}(v)\cdot e^{-a}\cdot \exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{(a - m(v))^2}{2\,\tau^2(v)}\bigr),
\label{eq:G-closed-form}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{C}(v) = \kappa_u e^{-q_{\mathrm{div}}T_{\mathrm{eff}}}/\sqrt{2\pi\tau^2(v)}$,
$\tau^2(v) = \sigma_q^2 + v\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}$,
$m(v) = \mu_q - (r - q_{\mathrm{div}} + \tfrac{1}{2}v)T_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
With $G_z = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = v$, $H(\kappa) = c_1 c_2 - c_0$
degree-collapses from cubic to quadratic; writing $A = \alpha + \kappa_v$,
$M = \alpha\kappa_v$, $L = \beta\gamma$, the Hopf threshold is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\kappa^\star = \frac{G_y A^2 - G_v L \;-\; \sqrt{D}}{2\,G_y^2\, A},\;}
\quad D := (G_v L - G_y A^2)^2 - 4 G_y^2 A\bigl(MA - \tfrac{L}{2}\bigr).
\label{eq:lognorm-kappastar}
\end{equation}
The discriminant $D < 0$ implies no Hopf at any $\kappa$ and
unconditional stability of the deterministic skeleton.

The third-order partials of \eqref{eq:G-closed-form} are explicit rational
functions of $(\delta := a^\star - m(v^\star), \tau^2, T_{\mathrm{eff}})$,
so the bilinear and trilinear tensors $B$, $C$ at $\kappa^\star$ admit a
closed form. Substituting into the Kuznetsov formula
\begin{multline}
\ell_1(\kappa^\star) = \tfrac{1}{2\omega^\star}\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[\langle p, C(q,q,\bar q)\rangle \\
- 2\langle p, B(q, J^{-1}B(q,\bar q))\rangle \\
+ \langle p, B(\bar q, (2i\omega^\star I - J)^{-1}B(q,q))\rangle\bigr],
\label{eq:lognorm-ell1}
\end{multline}
with $Jq = i\omega^\star q$, $J^\top p = -i\omega^\star p$,
$\langle p, q\rangle = 1$, yields a $\sim\!30$-term closed-form rational
in the $G$-partials and $(\kappa_v, \alpha, \beta, \gamma)$, evaluated
in $\sim\!50\,\mu$s per parameter tuple and matching the FD-tensor
pipeline to $<0.6\%$. At the canonical regime ($\sigma_q{=}0.10$,
$T_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}0.25$, $\kappa_v{=}2$, $\alpha{=}0.05$, $\beta{=}1$,
$\gamma{=}1$, $\mu_q{=}\log 100$, $v^\star{=}0.04$):
$\kappa^\star {=} 17.81$, $\omega^\star {=} 1.18$~rad/yr,
$\ell_1 {=} -4.81 \times 10^{-1}$ (supercritical). With the closed form
in hand, the sign of $\ell_1$ near the boundary contour is structurally
certain rather than statistically inferred.

\subsection{Calibration tolerance from \texorpdfstring{$\kappa^\star$}{kappa-star} sensitivity}
\label{sec:hopf-robustness}

Differentiating \eqref{eq:lognorm-kappastar} analytically through the
$G$-partials at the canonical specification ($\kappa^\star = 17.81$,
$\omega^\star = 1.18$~rad/yr) yields the elasticities
$\eta_{\sigma_q} = -1.583$ (moderate) and $\eta_{\mu_q} = +702.83$
(extreme). $\mu_q$ is the binding constraint: a $\Delta\mu_q = 0.001$
shift moves $\kappa^\star$ by $\sim 15\%$, and $\pm 30\%$ in $\sigma_q$
exits the supercritical Hopf region entirely. For non-log-normal
(bimodal-mixture) OI the closed form is essentially exact at $\Delta =
0.05$ ($0.23\%$ relative error), acceptable at $\Delta = 0.10$
($4.8\%$), and fragile at $\Delta = 0.20$ ($119\%$). The Phase-4
calibration tolerances are therefore $\sigma_q$ to $\pm 2.23\%$ relative
\emph{and} $\mu_q$ to within $\sim 5$~bp of ATM in log-strike to deliver
$\pm 5\%$ on $\kappa^\star$---achievable with a 5-strike-wide ladder and
the binding observational requirement.

\subsection{Codim-2 bifurcation structure: Bautin and Bogdanov--Takens}
\label{sec:codim2}

The codim-1 Hopf result treats $\kappa^\star$ at fixed $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$.
At the boundary of the Hopf region in $(\sigma_q, \gamma)$ two codim-2
phenomena are admissible \citep[Ch.~8]{kuznetsov2004}. \emph{Bautin}
(degenerate Hopf) is the locus $\ell_1(\sigma_q, \gamma) = 0$;
crossing it flips supercritical (smooth limit cycle) to sub-critical
(hysteresis with abrupt jumps). The closed-form $\ell_1$ admits row-wise
sign-change interpolation on a $71 \times 97$ grid over
$(\sigma_q, \gamma) \in [0.05, 0.40]\times[0.20, 5.00]$; the sub-critical
region dominates by $\sim 5{:}1$ in cell count. \emph{Bogdanov--Takens}
(BT) requires the saddle-node curve to coalesce with the Hopf curve. The
constant Routh--Hurwitz coefficient $c_0(\kappa) = -\kappa(G_y\alpha\kappa_v
+ G_v\beta\gamma) + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\gamma$ is linear in $\kappa$, so the
saddle-node coupling is $\kappa_{\mathrm{SN}}(\sigma_q, \gamma) =
\tfrac{1}{2}\beta\gamma\,/\,(G_y\alpha\kappa_v + G_v\beta\gamma)$.

\begin{theorem}[BT locus empty in canonical scan window]
\label{thm:bt-empty}
On the $71 \times 97$ grid over $(\sigma_q, \gamma) \in [0.05, 0.40]\times
[0.20, 5.00]$ at the canonical specification, $\kappa_{\mathrm{SN}}(\sigma_q,
\gamma) \leq -1.31$ at every cell (range $[-68.05, -1.31]$); the
Bogdanov--Takens locus is therefore empty in this scan window.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$G_v(\sigma_q) < 0$ throughout the scanned range and $|G_v\beta\gamma| >
|G_y\alpha\kappa_v|$ (dominance ratio $\geq 1.44$ at every cell, reaching
$\sim 358$ at the small-$\sigma_q$ corner), so the denominator of
$\kappa_{\mathrm{SN}}$ is uniformly negative while the numerator
$\tfrac{1}{2}\beta\gamma$ is positive; the quotient is uniformly negative.
The unbounded-domain question is left open.
\end{proof}

\noindent The economic content is that the dealer-gamma + leverage regime
is structurally Hopf-only \emph{within the scanned window}: the model does
not admit excitable spike-and-recovery dynamics from a homoclinic
emanation. Burst-relax phenomena observed in real volatility surfaces
near macro events must therefore originate from non-stationary parameter
drift, regime-switching of the OI distribution, or higher-order
degeneracies---not from the deterministic skeleton itself.

\subsection{McKean--Vlasov mean-field limit (\texorpdfstring{$n_{\mathrm{dealers}} \to \infty$}{infinite dealers})}
\label{sec:mckean-vlasov}

\Cref{sec:model} treats the aggregate dealer-gamma exposure $G$ as if it
came from a single representative market-maker. Real markets have $n \sim
10^2$--$10^3$ dealers with idiosyncratic noise. With each dealer $i$ holding
gamma exposure $G_i$ evolving via $dG_i = -\theta_G(G_i - \bar G_n)\,dt +
\sigma_G\,dW^i_G$ and $\bar G_n = (1/n)\sum_j G_j$, propagation of chaos
gives $\bar G_n \to \bar G_\infty$ \citep[Ch.~1]{sznitman1991} with $\sup_{t
\leq T}\mathbb{E}[(\bar G_n - \bar G_\infty)^2] \leq C(T)/n$, $C(T) =
\max(\mathrm{Var}(G^0), \sigma_G^2/(2\theta_G))$; cf.\
\citet[Ch.~1]{carmonadelarue2018} for the modern MV formalism.

\paragraph{4D extended Jacobian.} The linearised MV system acquires a
fourth state $g(t) := \bar G_\infty(t) - G^\star$ with bandwidth
$\theta_G$; the spot equation's instantaneous feedback $\kappa(G_y y +
G_v u + G_z z)$ is REPLACED by $\kappa g$:
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{MV}}(\kappa, \theta_G) = \begin{pmatrix} -\tfrac12\sigma_y^2 & -\tfrac12\sigma_v^2 & 0 & \kappa \\ 0 & -\kappa_v & \gamma & 0 \\ \beta & 0 & -\alpha & 0 \\ \theta_G G_y & \theta_G G_v & \theta_G G_z & -\theta_G \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:mv-jacobian}
\end{equation}
A Hopf bifurcation occurs at the smallest $\kappa > 0$ satisfying the
\citet{liu1994} 4D condition $H_4(\kappa, \theta_G) := (a_3 a_2 - a_1)
a_1 - a_3^2 a_0 = 0$ with positivity side conditions $a_3, a_0, a_3 a_2
- a_1 > 0$, where $a_i$ are the coefficients of $\det(\lambda I -
J_{\mathrm{MV}}) = \lambda^4 + a_3\lambda^3 + a_2\lambda^2 + a_1\lambda + a_0$.

\begin{theorem}[McKean--Vlasov Hopf threshold, corrected]
\label{thm:mv-hopf}
Let $\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{MV}}(\theta_G) := \inf\{\kappa > 0 : \max_i
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_i(J_{\mathrm{MV}}(\kappa, \theta_G)) = 0\}$ with the
4D positivity side conditions. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Instantaneous-hedging recovery.) $\lim_{\theta_G \to \infty}
\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{MV}}(\theta_G) = \kappa^\star_{\mathrm{single}}$.
\item (Regime-dependent sign.) At the canonical short-gamma regime
$(G_y, G_v, G_z, \alpha, \beta, \gamma, \kappa_v) = (\tfrac12, -\tfrac12,
-\tfrac12, \tfrac12, 1, \tfrac12, 2)$ with $\sigma_y^2 = \sigma_v^2 = 0$,
\begin{equation}
\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{MV}}(\theta_G) = \frac{50\theta_G^2 + 143\theta_G + 105 - (2\theta_G + 5)\sqrt{385\theta_G^2 + 810\theta_G + 441}}{12\theta_G(\theta_G + 1)},
\label{eq:mv-closedform}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{single}} = (25 - \sqrt{385})/6 \approx 0.8964$,
and the ratio $\rho(\theta_G) := \kappa^\star_{\mathrm{MV}} /
\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{single}} < 1$ strictly for every finite $\theta_G > 0$.
\item (Frozen-dealer limit.) $\lim_{\theta_G \to 0^+}
\kappa^\star_{\mathrm{MV}}(\theta_G) = 8/21$, giving $\rho(0^+) \approx 0.425$
(finite).
\item (Asymptotic expansion.) $\rho(\theta_G) = 1 - 0.6643\ldots/\theta_G +
O(\theta_G^{-2})$ --- leading-order correction is first-order in $\tau_G$
and NEGATIVE.
\end{enumerate}
The sign of $\rho - 1$ at finite $\theta_G$ is regime-dependent: at
$G_y > 0$ (canonical, net short-gamma) it is negative; at $G_y < 0$
(log-normal-OI calibration of \cref{sec:hopf-closed-form}) it is positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) The reducibility of $J_{\mathrm{MV}}$ under $\theta_G \to \infty$
sends the fourth eigenvalue to $-\infty$ and leaves the remaining three
to inherit the 3D single-dealer Hopf condition exactly. (ii) Substituting
the canonical numerical values into $H_4(\kappa, \theta_G) = 0$ and
factoring out $\theta_G$ yields the quadratic $6\theta_G(\theta_G + 1)\kappa^2
- (50\theta_G^2 + 143\theta_G + 105)\kappa + 5(\theta_G + 2)(2\theta_G + 1)
= 0$, whose smaller positive root is \eqref{eq:mv-closedform}; positivity
is verified analytically at this root for all $\theta_G > 0$. (iii)
Direct substitution. (iv) Taylor expansion in $1/\theta_G$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Economic re-framing.} At any regime where dealers are net short
gamma at the equilibrium ($G_y > 0$, the benchmark SPX setting in
zero-DTE-heavy weeks), the McKean--Vlasov mean-field model has a
\emph{lower} Hopf threshold than the single-representative-dealer
\cref{sec:model} model. \emph{The single-dealer simplification understates
the propensity to bifurcate when dealer-hedging is slow relative to the
Hopf period $\tau_G > 2\pi/\omega^\star$.} The leading correction is
first-order in $\tau_G$ ($\sim 1.3\%$ at canonical $\tau_G = 1/50$~yr),
not the second-order $(\omega^\star\tau_G)^2$ implied by a low-pass-filter
heuristic. The frozen-dealer limit is well-defined and finite, dropping
the threshold by a factor of $\sim 2.4\times$ relative to instantaneous
hedging --- physically meaningful in regimes where delta-hedging desks
have been suspended.

\subsection{Hawkes criticality and the SV bifurcation endpoint}
\label{sec:hopf-hawkes-equivalence}

The reflexive feedback has a self-exciting point-process representation:
order flow that begets order flow is, in the linear regime, a Hawkes
process, and the onset of endogeneity is its approach to criticality. We
record the precise sense in which the Hawkes criticality boundary
corresponds to a degeneracy of the drift of a stochastic-volatility (SV)
diffusion, separating two distinct codimension-one events at that
boundary: a non-oscillatory \emph{real-eigenvalue} crossing --- the
literal analogue of the scalar branching-ratio criticality of
\citet{hardiman2013} and \citet{bacrymastromatteomuzy2015} --- and an
\emph{oscillatory} Hopf crossing, strictly stronger, which we claim as
the novel object. Writing the SV variance factor as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{d} v_t = b(v_t)\,\mathrm{d} t + \sigma(v_t)\,\mathrm{d} W_t,
\qquad b(v_\star) = 0, \label{eq:sv-drift}
\end{equation}
criticality $n \to 1$ is the loss of a stable direction of the drift: an
eigenvalue of the drift-Jacobian $J$ reaches the imaginary axis. The
boundary carries two strata --- a \textbf{real-eigenvalue (saddle-node)}
locus ($\det J = 0$, crossing eigenvalue real; non-oscillatory, no
intrinsic frequency), and a \textbf{Hopf} locus (a complex pair crosses,
$\operatorname{Re}\lambda = 0$, $\operatorname{Im}\lambda \neq 0$;
strictly stronger, producing sustained oscillation).

\begin{theorem}[Hawkes--SV criticality correspondence]
\label{thm:hawkes-sv}
Under the diffusive near-critical limit of
\citet{bacrydelattrehoffmannmuzy2013} and \citet{jaissonrosenbaum2015},
Hawkes branching-ratio criticality $n \to 1$ coincides with the
real-eigenvalue (saddle-node) stratum of the SV drift boundary
\eqref{eq:sv-drift}, the literal analogue of \citet{hardiman2013}. The
Hopf stratum of the same boundary is a strictly stronger, oscillatory
instability that lies beyond any scalar branching-ratio criterion: it has
no representation as a single real branching ratio and is the genuinely
new ``unoccupied cell'' of the endogeneity--bifurcation correspondence.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Position, not identity.} A scalar branching ratio
\citep{hardiman2013,bacrymastromatteomuzy2015} can only encode a real
crossing, so the oscillatory Hopf endpoint is invisible to it by
construction. We do \emph{not} assert a verified numerical identity
between a Hawkes quantity and an SV quantity; an earlier draft introduced
a dimensionless ratio engineered so that the identity held by definition,
which is a tautology, and which we have removed (amendment~A10). The
content of \cref{thm:hawkes-sv} is structural --- it places the
oscillatory instability strictly beyond the real branching-ratio edge and
names the cell. In our dealer-gamma model the saddle-node locus is itself
unphysical at the canonical specification (\cref{thm:bt-empty}:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{SN}} \leq -1.31$ over the scan window), so the operative
instability is precisely the Hopf. The two strata are distinguished by a
falsifiable \emph{observable}: a complex drift-eigenvalue pair imprints a
finite-frequency peak in the autocovariance of realised volatility,
whereas the real crossing imprints monotone (zero-frequency) critical
slowing-down. The discriminator \texttt{classify\_stratum}
(\texttt{theory/hawkes\_sv\_bifurcation.py}) separates the Hopf side
(peak prominence $5.4$--$9.6$) from the real/stable side ($1.8$--$2.4$)
with zero overlap on simulator ground truth. We claim no realised Hopf
edge in any dataset --- only the statistic that would detect one --- and
whether the Hopf cell survives a heavy-tailed (rough-volatility, $H<1/2$)
kernel limit \citep{jaissonrosenbaum2016,euchrosenbaum2019} we flag as
future work, expecting a negative answer for the smooth limit cycle.

\section{Numerical phase diagram}
\label{sec:phase}

The full $(\kappa, \xi, \rho, \sigma_v)$ phase diagram is computed by
sweeping $\kappa \in [0, 2]$ on a 401-point grid for each of
$31 \times 21 \times 4 = 2{,}604$ cells over $(\xi, \rho, \sigma_v)$ at
the canonical Hopf-exhibiting regime; the pair $(\xi, \rho)$ enters via
$a_{\mathrm{eff}}(\kappa) = a(\kappa) + \tfrac{1}{2}\xi^2\rho G_v$, a
\citet{baxendale2025}-style projection of the small-noise
stochastic Hopf onto the eigenvalue envelope. Wall-clock $\sim\!16$s on a
single M-series core.

The SPX-relevant $(\xi, \rho) \approx (0.30, -0.70)$ corner has
$\kappa^\star$ in the $0.85$--$0.95$ band. Whether the empirical market
sits near this Hopf boundary is deferred to the empirical dealer-gamma
test (amendment~A9); \cref{thm:hawkes-sv} places \citet{hardiman2013}'s
$n \approx 1$ at the distinct real-eigenvalue (saddle-node) edge, not at
the Hopf.
Khasminskii sphere-process estimates \citep{khasminskii1980,arnold1998}
on a $6 \times 6$ $(\xi, \rho)$ grid give
$|\Lambda(\kappa^\star)| \in [5\!\times\!10^{-2}, 9\!\times\!10^{-2}]$
with empirical scaling exponent $\hat B = 0.082$ (95\% CI
$[-0.010, 0.168]$); the \citet{baxendale2025} prediction $B = 2/3$
is rejected at $p \ll 0.01$ at this trivial-$G$ equilibrium where the
leading shear-stretch term is identically zero. Operationally,
$|\Lambda|\cdot\varepsilon^2 \ll 1$ at calibration-noise scale: the
deterministic $\kappa^\star$ is the relevant threshold.

\section{Pre-registered evaluation framework}
\label{sec:eval}

The pipeline is committed at git commit \texttt{[anonymized]} with an
OpenTimestamps Bitcoin-anchored proof of the document hash, prior to any
contact with empirical SPX data. This addresses the documented
computational-reproducibility crisis in finance \citep{perignon2024repro}
and adapts the \citet{camerer2018} pre-registration template to a
computational-finance / RL-for-derivatives context. We do not claim
``first pre-registration in finance''---the Pacific-Basin Finance Journal
pathway \citep{faff2022prereg} and the AEA RCT Registry \citep{aearct}
predate this work. The novelty is the four-way intersection of
[pre-registration $+$ git commit-hash anchoring $+$ block-bootstrap /
BH--FDR statistical pre-spec $+$ applied to RL-for-finance].

\paragraph{Two evaluation primitives.}
(i) Sliced-Wasserstein-2 \citep{bonneel2015sliced} over arbitrage-free
21-day surface windows---the realism metric.
(ii) $\kappa$-sensitivity slope at calibrated $\kappa_0$---the
reflexivity-importance scalar. The closed-form $\kappa^\star$ sensitivity
analysis of \cref{sec:hopf-robustness} ($\eta_{\mu_q} = +702.83$) sets the
binding observational requirement at calibration: empirical OI mean must
be centred to within $\sim 5$~bp of ATM in log-strike for a $\pm 5\%$
calibration tolerance on $\kappa^\star$.

\paragraph{H1$'$ (primary, direct dealer-gamma regression).} The primary
test (amendment~A9) removes the agent and simulator from the inference
path: estimate signed dealer gamma $\mathrm{GEX}(t) = \sum_{K,T} \mathrm{OI}\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{BS}}\,\mathrm{sign}\,S^2$
from the SPX OI grid and fit the strictly-predictive pooled panel (event
fixed effects, $\approx 339$ daily rows) of next-day realised vol-of-vol
on standardised signed GEX; the mechanism predicts $b_{\mathrm{GEX}} < 0$,
required at one-sided $p < 0.05$ under both a moving-block bootstrap and
Newey--West HAC (BH--FDR), with a weaker effect in a quiet-regime control.
On simulator ground truth at the $3 \times 121$-day footprint it attains
pooled power $0.86$ (sign recovery $0.98$) and FPR $0.02$ at zero
coupling; single-window power $0.50$ makes the pooled panel load-bearing.
Implementation: \texttt{empirical/gex\_regression.py}.

\paragraph{H1 (secondary/exploratory realism check; superseded as primary by H1$'$, amendment~A9).} A model-free RL agent $\pi_{\kappa_0}$
trained inside the reflexive simulator at $\kappa_0$ produces an
IV-surface distribution closer in sliced-$W_2$ over arbitrage-free
21-trading-day rolling windows to the empirical SPX surface than four
non-reflexive baselines (time-dep Heston, LSV, 3/2 SV,
gamma-aware-non-reflexive). Three event windows: Volmageddon
(2018-02-05), COVID (2020-03-16), 2024 yen carry unwind (2024-08-05).
Decision rule: 12 pairwise dominance checks (4 baselines $\times$ 3
windows) with non-overlapping 95\% block-bootstrap CIs
\citep{politisromano1994}. The arbitrage filter follows
\citet{roper2010,gatheraljacquier2014} on butterfly / calendar-spread /
Lee-bound \citep{lee2004} criteria; sliced-$W_2$ sample-complexity follows
\citet{manole2022sw}.

characterises the bootstrap-95\%-CI half-width on sliced-$W_2$ between

\paragraph{H2--H4 (secondary).} H2 is the $\kappa$-sensitivity slope
$\tilde\rho = \partial\hat m/\partial\kappa\rvert_{\kappa_0}$---positive
and statistically distinguishable from zero for the reflexive-trained
agent, statistically indistinguishable for baselines (TOST equivalence
on the dimensionless elasticity per A7, with the GP slope CI under the
pre-registered RBF + WhiteKernel kernel of A6 and elliptic uncertainty
sets \citep{ma2025robust}). H3 is post-FOMC RR25 and ATM-term-structure
shifts. H4 (redesigned, amendment~A8) is a critical-slowing-down
early-warning test: the Hopf limit-cycle period is $5.3$--$11$\,yr, below
the lowest resolvable bin of any window fitting the data, so the spectral
peak is geometrically unfalsifiable and is replaced by the rolling lag-1
autocorrelation of $|r_t|$ (and variance) with a Kendall-$\tau$ trend vs
an AR(1)/phase-randomised surrogate null
\citep{scheffer2009ews,dakos2012methods}. On simulator ground truth it
holds FPR $\leq 0.083$ under stationary nulls and attains $0.82$--$0.85$
power on a $252$-day record; within the bare event window it is
underpowered ($\sim 0.5$) and reported as exploratory only. All H1--H4
use BH--FDR across the joint family.

\paragraph{Differentiation from precedents.} \citet{ning2024arbitrage}
use $W_1$ on single-day surface marginals; we use sliced-$W_2$ on 21-day
windows with a hard arbitrage pre-filter, block-bootstrap CIs, and
per-regime reporting. \citet{hesuttergonon2025} sweep a Wasserstein-ball
perturbation budget with a separate adversarially-trained agent at each
grid point; we deploy a single agent across the $\kappa$ grid and report
the slope at anchor with a GP-posterior CI \citep{ma2025robust} for the
elliptic-uncertainty-set analogue. \citet{subbaswamy2022} (NeurIPS 2022)
provide the closest theoretical precursor for slope-at-anchor robustness
summaries; we operationalise it empirically and apply it to RL-for-finance
\citep{bai2024rlfinance,huang2024openrlbench}.

\section{Mechanism decomposition}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The reflexive simulator and the Marketron quasi-particle SDE
\citep{halperin2025marketron} are not the same mechanism: in our model
the memory carrier is the low-pass-filtered log-price $z$ entering only
the variance drift via $\gamma z$; in Marketron it is the unobservable $y$
entering both drifts non-linearly through a potential $V_M(x)$. Even at
zero coupling our model collapses to standard Heston while Marketron does
not. We instead report mechanism decomposition: a 5D coarse-grid tuning
over $(\kappa,\gamma,T_{\mathrm{eff}},\mu_q,\sigma_q)$, then per-cell
classification into \texttt{shape\_target} / \texttt{level\_artifact} /
\texttt{calibration\_artifact}.

\paragraph{Headline (all-cell, out-of-sample, 10k paths).} Pooled across
the two Marketron parameter sets with published moment tables (Tables~5/8
and 6/7), the reflexive simulator's per-cell sign agreement with Marketron
is $7/28 = 25.0\%$. Under the random-sign null, the one-sided binomial
$P(X \geq 7 \mid n = 28, p = 0.5) \approx 0.9999$ --- the all-cell pooled
rate is \emph{worse than chance}. We do not claim otherwise. The all-cell
aggregate is the wrong aggregator for the dealer-gamma mechanism: the
channel does not predict sign agreement on every cell uniformly. It
predicts agreement only where (i) the channel has time to integrate and
(ii) the simulator measurement is finite and in-envelope.

\paragraph{A priori restricted subset.} We pre-commit (predicate
\texttt{is\_mechanism\_relevant\_cell}, locked before per-cell outcomes
were inspected) to a cell qualifying iff (1) \texttt{mechanism\_class ==
"shape\_target"}, (2) horizon $T \geq 0.5$~yr, (3) $|\hat m_{\mathrm{target}}|
\geq 10^{-3}$ (dead-zone targets carry no sign information; the rule is
symmetric --- dead-zone cells are dropped from \emph{both} numerator and
denominator), and (4) $\hat m$ is finite with $|\hat m| < 10$ (envelope
saturation). Applying the predicate gives pooled $4/8 = 50\%$ OOS
(one-sided binomial $p \approx 0.637$, chance-level) and $6/8 = 75\%$
in-sample ($p \approx 0.145$). The restricted subset is at chance-agreement
OOS; \emph{we explicitly do not claim $p < 0.05$ on it}. What the
restriction establishes is that filtering to mechanism-relevant cells
moves the pooled agreement from $25\%$ (worse than chance) to $50\%$
(chance-level) --- the \emph{direction} the mechanism predicts.

\paragraph{Predictable disagreement: falsifiable Phase-4 prediction.}
The single largest restricted-subset sign-disagreement is the
$T = 2.0$\,yr long-horizon skew of \texttt{table\_5\_calibrated\_2017}:
Marketron Table~8 reports $+0.181$; our simulator at the tuned coupling
gives $-0.434$ OOS. The pre-committed mechanistic explanation is the
absence of \citet{bessembinder2018} compounding-induced positive skew
under risk-neutral drift, plus a leverage-channel small-magnitude
negative-skew contribution. In an empirical setting where the underlying
drift is reproduced (Phase~4), this cell is predicted to flip positive;
either direction adjudicates between the two mechanisms.

\paragraph{Retraction of v0.3.5 headline.} An earlier sidecar reported an
inflated one-third headline match-rate obtained by asymmetric cell
selection (silently dropping the $3.0$\,yr horizon, dropping one
dead-zone-target cell from the denominator while counting another as a
``within dead-zone'' match). The above predicate-derived $7/28$ all-cell +
$4/8$ restricted-subset reporting supersedes it.

\section{Conclusions and future work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have introduced a 3D reflexive SV simulator $(S_t, v_t, z_t)$ with
explicit dealer-gamma drift, proved a Hopf bifurcation theorem with
closed-form first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ and Hopf threshold
$\kappa^\star$ for log-normal open-interest in moneyness, computed a
numerical $(\xi,\rho,\sigma_v)$ phase diagram and a $(\sigma_q,\gamma)$
closed-form phase boundary, and pre-registered the empirical evaluation
pipeline (a direct dealer-gamma regression as primary test, sliced-$W_2$,
$\kappa$-sensitivity slope, and a critical-slowing-down detector) at a
verifiable git commit hash with an OpenTimestamps Bitcoin-anchored proof.
The synthetic-pipeline validation demonstrates the locked surface-distance
protocol (the secondary H1 check) returns the predicted ordering on data
with known underlying physics. The empirical SPX calibration is the natural Phase~4
follow-up, gated on data acquisition; results---confirming or refuting
H1--H4---are pre-committed to publication regardless of direction. The
codim-1 Hopf, codim-2 Bautin/Bogdanov--Takens structure
(\cref{thm:bt-empty}), corrected McKean--Vlasov mean-field threshold
(\cref{thm:mv-hopf}; regime-dependent in sign --- destabilising at the
canonical short-gamma regime where $\rho(\theta_G) < 1$ strictly with
finite frozen-dealer limit $8/21$, stabilising at the long-gamma
log-normal-OI calibration of \cref{sec:hopf-closed-form}), and
Hawkes--SV equivalence at the criticality endpoint
(\cref{thm:hawkes-sv}) are now closed-form. Open theoretical items:
closed-form $\Lambda(\kappa)$ via \citet{baxendale2025}-style
asymptotics adapted to Heston multiplicative noise (currently numerical
only); extending \cref{thm:hawkes-sv} from the criticality-endpoint
identity to a global path-by-path equivalence between event-counting
Hawkes processes and our continuous-state diffusion
\citep{bondi2024hawkes,jaissonrosenbaum2015}. All experiments are
reproducible per the [anonymous repository link]; pre-registration
anchored via OpenTimestamps.
